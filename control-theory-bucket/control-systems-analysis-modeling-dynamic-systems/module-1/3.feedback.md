# Why Use Feedback?

**Experimental Data:**

- On level road at 65 mph, a 1 degree change in throttle angle causes a 10 mph change in speed.

- A 1% change in road grade yields a 5 mph change in speed.

## Open-Loop Control:

### No assumed modeling error:

$y = 10(u - 0.5w) \rightarrow y= 10(\frac{r}{10} - 0.5w)$

$y = r - 5w$

### With modeling error:

$y = 9(u - 0.5w) \rightarrow y= 9(\frac{r}{10} - 0.5w)$

$y = \frac{9}{10}r - 4.5w$

## Closed-Loop Control or Feedback Control

###  No assumed modeling error:

$y_c = 10[u - 0.5w] \rightarrow y_c = 10[10(r-y_c)-0.5w]$

$y_c = \frac{100}{101} r - \frac{5}{101}w$

### With modeling error:

$y_c = 9[u - 0.5w] \rightarrow y_c = 9[10(r-y_c)-0.5w]$

$y_c = \frac{90}{91} r - \frac{4.5}{91}w$

**Note:** Arbritarily increasing control gain for steady state systems may work, but for dynamic systems, increasing control gain may cause instability (ex. squeakiness caused by loud speakers)

**Summary:**

- Open-loop systems don't use measurements of the system behavior.

- Closed-loop control systems do use feedback measurements of the system behavior and lead to more sensitibity to modeling errors and disturbances.
    - Including dynamics which makes systems more complex
